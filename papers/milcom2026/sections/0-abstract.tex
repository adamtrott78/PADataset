RF sensing can provide early evidence for cyberspace windows of superiority, but closed-set RF classifiers are unsafe when adversaries emit signal behaviors outside the trained taxonomy. In this paper, we study open-world RF preliminary-action detection: the task of recognizing known RF behaviors while rejecting novel behaviors as unknown. We introduce DQNGuard, a budgeted open-set decision layer that operates on a multi-domain preliminary-action backbone using predicted-class calibration, variance/energy guard evidence, and known-only thresholding. The system maps OTA RF windows into IQ, FFT, DCT, and polar representations, processes them with a preliminary-action CNN, and then routes each window to either known preliminary-action evidence or an unknown behavior pool. We evaluate DQNGuard on over-the-air WiFi, Bluetooth, and Zigbee captures spanning five preliminary-action classes: Scan, Burst, Sustain, Hop, and Replay. Under fixed Scan-surrogate known-budget calibration, DQNGuard improves unknown-class detection relative to VarMax and a DQN-IDS-style comparison while maintaining low known-class rejection. We further evaluate a Target--Surrogate Matrix and show that surrogate-open calibration is highly target-dependent: withheld calibration classes are not interchangeable proxies for unknown behavior. These results position DQNGuard as an RF sensing and triage layer for QR-CWoS pipelines, where known preliminary actions can support downstream ATT\&CK/EW hypotheses and rejected unknowns can feed label-making and continual-learning workflows.

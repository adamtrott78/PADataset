Open-set recognition (OSR) studies classifiers that must preserve recognition of known classes while identifying samples outside the known taxonomy. Scheirer et al. formalized this problem through open-space risk, and Bendale and Boult extended OSR to deep networks with OpenMax~\cite{scheirer2013toward,bendale2016openmax}. Calibration and energy-based uncertainty work further shows why closed-set softmax scores can remain overconfident under distribution shift~\cite{guo2017calibration,liu2020energy,wang2021eow}. These works motivate our use of confidence, variance-style, and energy-style evidence, but our focus is a budgeted RF decision layer rather than generic image-domain OOD detection.

The closest methodological predecessor is varMax. Baye et al. introduced varMax as a confidence-based zero-day recognition method using maximum-score and variance-style evidence to reject unknown samples~\cite{baye2024varmax}. Broggi et al. further develop the uncertainty and novelty-management perspective behind varMax~\cite{broggi2025varmax}. DQNGuard inherits this view that novelty can be detected from closed-set confidence geometry, but conditions calibration on the predicted class and selects the operating threshold under an explicit known-rejection budget.

A second line of work treats OSR as a decision process. Tiwari et al. propose DQN-IDS, a reinforcement-learning approach for open-set-enabled intrusion detection~\cite{tiwaridqn}. We do not claim deployment-time continual reinforcement learning. Instead, we evaluate a DQN-IDS-style confidence head as a comparison point and use DQNGuard as a calibrated decision layer over fixed backbone outputs.

RF and spectrum-sensing systems motivate the operational setting. Searchlight shows that practical RF sensing must detect signals under receiver impairments, congestion, burstiness, and OTA hardware effects~\cite{bell2023searchlight}. Stitching the Spectrum emphasizes that OTA RF learning systems must address real capture conditions and signal localization rather than relying only on simulation~\cite{uvaydov2024stitching}. These systems do not solve preliminary-action OSR, but they clarify the kind of RF front end needed by downstream cyber/EMS pipelines.

Open-set RF and electromagnetic signal analysis has also explored multi-domain representations. Wei et al. exploit IQ, FFT, and DCT domains for detecting unclassified electromagnetic signals~\cite{wei2024multidomain}. Trott et al. similarly study RF modulation classifiers in the presence of novel samples and motivate multi-domain RF OSR~\cite{trott2026model}. The present work adopts this principle, but exposes logits, softmax probabilities, predicted labels, and intermediate features to a budgeted OSR decision layer rather than relying on a single representation or a separate novelty model.

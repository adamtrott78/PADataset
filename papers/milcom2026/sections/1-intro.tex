Modern military operations increasingly depend on decisions made across cyberspace and the electromagnetic spectrum (EMS). An RF behavior observed before, during, or around a cyber operation can provide early evidence that an adversary is preparing to scan, interfere, replay, sustain access, or shift emissions. In the Quantifiable and Resilient Cyberspace Windows of Superiority (QR-CWoS) setting, this evidence matters because the value of a response depends on timing: a defender must recognize useful precursor behavior before the opportunity to act has closed.

A closed-set RF classifier is poorly matched to this setting. Standard classifiers assign every input to one of the known classes, and softmax confidence provides no native mechanism for saying that an over-the-air (OTA) RF window does not belong to any known behavior. In contested RF environments, this assumption is unsafe: jammers, spoofers, replay emitters, cooperative radios, protocol variants, and hardware/channel effects can all produce behaviors outside the available training set. The most dangerous failure is a confident but wrong assignment that routes a genuinely novel behavior into the nearest known class exactly when downstream systems should preserve ambiguity.

Open-set recognition (OSR) addresses this failure mode by retaining known-class recognition while rejecting samples that do not conform to the learned decision structure. Prior confidence-based OSR work, including varMax~\cite{baye2024varmax,broggi2025varmax}, shows that classifier outputs such as top-score gaps, logit variance, and energy-style evidence can expose unfamiliar inputs without retraining the backbone. RF OSR work further shows that novelty can depend strongly on signal representation and on the composition of known and unknown classes~\cite{trott2026model}. DQN-IDS-style decision heads also motivate treating OSR as a decision process over confidence states~\cite{tiwaridqn}. Together, these results suggest that an OSR layer can serve as a useful RF front end for cyber/EMS reasoning systems, provided it controls known-sample rejection while preserving candidate unknowns for later analysis.

We study this problem for RF preliminary actions (PAs). A PA is an observable RF behavior that may precede, enable, or contextualize a later cyber, EMS, or cyber-physical effect. The evaluated PA classes are Scan, Burst, Sustain, Hop, and Replay. These labels are intentionally not final ATT\&CK or EW technique labels; they are precursor-level evidence. Known PA detections can feed downstream attack-chain or response-planning modules, while rejected unknowns form a behavior pool for label-making and continual RF behavior discovery.

This paper introduces DQNGuard, a budgeted OSR decision layer for OTA RF preliminary-action recognition. DQNGuard operates on a multi-domain PA backbone that maps RF windows into IQ, FFT, DCT, and polar representations and produces closed-set logits, probabilities, intermediate features, and a predicted PA. The decision layer applies predicted-class conditional calibration, variance/energy-style guard evidence, and known-only budgeted thresholding so that unknown detection is constrained by an explicit known-rejection budget rather than by an unconstrained search for novelty.

We evaluate DQNGuard on OTA WiFi, Bluetooth, and Zigbee captures and compare it against VarMax and a DQN-IDS-style confidence head. The goal is not to claim a complete QR-CWoS response system or final semantic labeling system. Rather, we ask whether an RF front end can separate known preliminary-action evidence from nonconforming unknown behavior under controlled calibration assumptions.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We formulate open-world RF preliminary-action detection as a sensing layer for QR-CWoS, where known PA detections provide precursor evidence and rejected unknowns provide a pool for downstream label-making.
    \item We introduce DQNGuard, a budgeted OSR decision layer combining predicted-class calibration, variance/energy guard evidence, and known-only thresholding.
    \item We evaluate DQNGuard on OTA captures spanning three protocol families and five PA behaviors under fixed-surrogate, Target--Surrogate Matrix, and target-open calibration regimes.
    \item We show that surrogate-open calibration is target-dependent, motivating future work on principled surrogate selection and continual RF behavior discovery.
\end{enumerate}

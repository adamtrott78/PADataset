\subsection{Preliminary Actions as Technique Precursors}
\label{subsec:pa_precursors}

We consider a contested Cyber-EMS setting in which a defender observes a shared RF band and must identify early RF behaviors that may precede or enable later cyber/electromagnetic effects. We refer to these behaviors as \emph{preliminary actions}. A preliminary action is not itself the full attack technique; rather, it is an observable precursor, prerequisite, or enabling behavior that can populate an evidence layer for later ATT\&CK/EW technique prediction.

The evaluated preliminary-action classes are Scan, Burst, Sustain, Hop, and Replay. Scan represents discovery-like RF behavior. Burst consists of repeated short on-intervals separated by quiet gaps. Sustain corresponds to persistent occupied-channel behavior. Hop captures frequency-agile dwell and revisit patterns. Replay captures repeated waveform/template behavior. The core open-world challenge is that a defender may possess labeled data for some preliminary actions while an adversary emits a behavior outside the known training set. In that case, the detector should reject the window as \texttt{unknown} rather than force it into the nearest known class.

\subsection{Backbone Outputs and OSR Formulation}
\label{subsec:osr_formulation}

Let $x$ denote an OTA RF window after preprocessing and feature extraction. A closed-set backbone $f_{\theta}$ maps $x$ to logits
\begin{equation}
    z = f_{\theta}(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{K},
\end{equation}
where $K$ is the number of known classes in the current fold. The backbone also produces softmax probabilities $p=\mathrm{softmax}(z)$, a closed-set prediction $\hat{y}=\arg\max_k p_k$, and an intermediate feature representation $h(x)$.

A standard closed-set classifier always emits one of the $K$ known labels. In open-set recognition, the decision layer instead returns
\begin{equation}
    \hat{y}_{\mathrm{osr}} \in \{1,\ldots,K,\texttt{unknown}\}.
\end{equation}
The OSR layer therefore must preserve known-class recognition while identifying target-open samples that were excluded from closed-set training.

We use the following confidence and evidence quantities throughout the OSR heads:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $P_1 = \max_k p_k$, the maximum softmax probability;
    \item $P_1-P_2$, the gap between the largest and second-largest softmax probabilities;
    \item $H(p)=-\sum_k p_k\log p_k$, the softmax entropy;
    \item variance-style evidence derived from the logit or feature distribution;
    \item energy-style evidence derived from the classifier score geometry.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Baseline OSR Heads}
\label{subsec:baseline_osr}

We compare DQNGuard against two families of OSR decision heads.

\paragraph{VarMax.}
VarMax is the predecessor confidence-based OSR rule. It uses confidence structure in the classifier outputs, including maximum-score and variance-style evidence, to assign an unknownness score to each sample. A calibrated operating rule then accepts the closed-set prediction or rejects the sample as \texttt{unknown}. In this paper VarMax is used as a decision-layer baseline on top of the same backbone outputs whenever possible, so performance differences are attributable to the OSR rule rather than to the feature extractor.

\paragraph{DQN-IDS-style confidence head.}
DQN-IDS motivates a reinforcement-learning-style OSR head over softmax confidence states. The confidence state is
\begin{equation}
    s = (P_1,\; P_1-P_2,\; H(p)).
\end{equation}
A DQN agent is trained using high- and low-confidence validation anchors, centroid-guided rewards, replay memory, and $\epsilon$-greedy exploration to choose between known and unknown actions. In this paper, this head is treated as an OSR-head ablation. We do not claim deployment-time continual learning unless explicitly evaluated; the relevant comparison is validation-time DQN calibration followed by held-out test evaluation.

\subsection{DQNGuard}
\label{subsec:dqnguard}

DQNGuard is a three-stage open-set decision layer that uses predicted-class conditional calibration, variance/energy evidence, and known-only budgeted thresholding to reject samples that do not conform to the learned known-class decision structure.

\paragraph{Stage 1: predicted-class conditional calibration.}
Rather than fitting one global rejection model, DQNGuard conditions calibration on the class predicted by the backbone. For a calibration sample $x_i$, the backbone first predicts $\hat{y}_i$. DQNGuard then compares the sample only against calibration statistics associated with that predicted class. This is important because known classes can have different confidence, variance, and energy profiles; a score that is ordinary for one known class may be anomalous for another.

\paragraph{Stage 2: variance/energy guard evidence.}
For each predicted class, DQNGuard fits percentile bands over guard evidence computed from the backbone outputs. The implemented guard uses the top-two confidence gap, variance-style evidence, and energy-style evidence. Let $m_j(x)$ denote one such metric for sample $x$ and metric $j$. For predicted class $c$, DQNGuard estimates lower and upper calibration bounds
\begin{equation}
    \ell_{c,j} = Q_{\alpha}(m_j \mid \hat{y}=c),
    \qquad
    u_{c,j} = Q_{1-\alpha}(m_j \mid \hat{y}=c),
\end{equation}
where $Q_{\alpha}$ is a calibration percentile. Samples whose evidence lies outside the predicted-class bands receive larger unknown scores.

\paragraph{Stage 3: known-only budgeted thresholding.}
DQNGuard converts the guard evidence into a continuous unknown score $r(x)$. The final operating threshold is chosen using known calibration samples only. For a known-rejection budget $\beta$, the threshold is
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\beta} = Q_{1-\beta}\bigl(r(x) \mid x \in \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}^{\mathrm{known}}\bigr).
\end{equation}
The final prediction is
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_{\mathrm{osr}} =
\begin{cases}
\texttt{unknown}, & r(x) \geq \tau_{\beta},\\
\hat{y}, & r(x) < \tau_{\beta}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:dqnguard_decision}
\end{equation}
The main experiments use $\beta=0.05$, corresponding to a 5\% known-rejection budget. This budgeted rule prevents the OSR layer from improving unknown detection by simply rejecting a large fraction of known samples.

\subsection{Calibration Modes}
\label{subsec:method_calibration_modes}

The OSR decision layer can be calibrated under different information assumptions.

\paragraph{Surrogate-open calibration.}
In surrogate-open calibration, a non-target class is withheld from backbone training and used as open-set calibration evidence. The final DQNGuard operating threshold remains known-budgeted: surrogate-open samples may shape or diagnose the guard, but the deployed threshold is selected from known calibration scores.

\paragraph{Target-open calibration.}
In target-open calibration, a small labeled subset of the held-out target-open class is available during OSR calibration, while the backbone remains trained only on known classes. This is not treated as a deployment assumption. Instead, it is a diagnostic condition that estimates how much performance is recoverable when the calibration set contains examples from the actual held-out unknown class.

\paragraph{Target--Surrogate Matrix.}
To study surrogate transfer, we evaluate a Target--Surrogate Matrix. One class is withheld as the target unknown, one different class is used as the surrogate-open calibration class, and the remaining classes form the known set. This matrix allows us to measure whether a surrogate that is useful for one target unknown also transfers to others, and whether surrogate quality can be predicted from calibration or representation diagnostics.

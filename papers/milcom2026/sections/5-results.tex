The results answer two questions. Table~\ref{tab:main_osr_results} tests whether DQNGuard improves the fixed-budget OSR operating point. Fig.~\ref{fig:target_surrogate_matrix} tests whether surrogate-open calibration transfers across target unknown PA behaviors.

\subsection{Main Open-Set Performance}
\label{subsec:main_results}

Table~\ref{tab:main_osr_results} compares OSR decision layers under the fixed Scan-surrogate calibration setting. Each method receives the same surrogate-open information, so the comparison emphasizes the decision rule rather than surrogate choice.

\input{tables/main_results/main_osr_results_table}

DQNGuard achieves the strongest fixed-budget operating point: it obtains the highest unknown F1 and OSR macro F1 while maintaining the lowest known-rejection rate among the compared methods. VarMax surrogate-all obtains the highest unknown AUROC, indicating strong aggregate ranking separation, but its selected operating point has higher known rejection and lower unknown F1 than DQNGuard. The DQN-IDS-style confidence head is competitive but does not match DQNGuard, suggesting that softmax confidence states alone are insufficient for PA routing.

\subsection{Target--Surrogate Transfer}
\label{subsec:target_surrogate_results}

Figure~\ref{fig:target_surrogate_matrix} reports the Target--Surrogate Matrix for surrogate-open calibration. Rows are held-out target unknowns, columns are surrogate-open calibration classes, and cells report unknown-class F1. The diagonal is omitted because a target unknown cannot also serve as its own surrogate.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.88\columnwidth]{figures/target_surrogate_matrix/target_surrogate_unknown_f1_matrix.pdf}
    \caption{Target--Surrogate Matrix for surrogate-open calibration. Cells report unknown-class F1; bold entries mark the best surrogate for each target.}
    \label{fig:target_surrogate_matrix}
\end{figure}

The matrix shows that surrogate choice materially changes unknown detection. Scan is best detected with Hop as surrogate, Sustain/Hop/Replay are best detected with Scan as surrogate, and Burst is best detected with Replay as surrogate. Several cells produce near-zero unknown F1, showing that the failure mode is not simply insufficient threshold aggressiveness; calibration evidence can be mismatched to the target behavior. The matrix therefore functions as a deployment diagnostic for when additional calibration evidence or a different surrogate-selection policy is needed.

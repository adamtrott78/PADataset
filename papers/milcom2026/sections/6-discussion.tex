The results support three observations. First, RF preliminary-action OSR should be evaluated as an operating-point problem, not only as a ranking problem. Table~\ref{tab:main_osr_results} shows that DQNGuard improves unknown F1 and OSR macro F1 while maintaining low known rejection, preserving the known PA evidence stream. This distinction is important for RF sensing: rejecting too many known windows can make an unknown detector look strong while weakening the evidence stream that downstream modules need.

Second, surrogate-open calibration is useful but target-dependent. Figure~\ref{fig:target_surrogate_matrix} shows that surrogate classes are not interchangeable sources of generic ``unknownness'' evidence. Surrogate-open calibration should therefore be treated as a deployment assumption to test, not as a universal substitute for target-open calibration. The Target--Surrogate Matrix is useful for this purpose because it exposes which withheld behaviors transfer and which require additional calibration evidence.

Third, DQNGuard is a sensing and triage component. It does not infer a complete attack chain, select an EW response, or assign final semantic labels to all novel emissions. Its role is to decide whether an OTA RF window should be emitted as known PA evidence or preserved as a nonconforming behavior for later analysis.

The main limitation is surrogate selection. The Target--Surrogate Matrix shows that some surrogates transfer better than others, but the current experiments do not provide an automatic rule for choosing the best surrogate before the target unknown is observed. A practical deployment would require a conservative fixed-surrogate policy, pooled surrogate evidence, human-reviewed diagnostics, or a learned surrogate-selection rule trained across a broader behavior taxonomy.

A second limitation is scale. The corpus spans three protocol families and five PA behaviors, which exposes target-dependent surrogate transfer but does not characterize the full space of RF behavior novelty. Larger taxonomies would enable leave-multiple-out evaluation, pooled calibration studies, and stronger tests of whether the observed transfer asymmetries generalize.

Finally, the evaluation begins after RF window extraction and feature construction. Operational performance also depends on upstream burst detection, segmentation, synchronization, OTA capture quality, and downstream label-making or response-policy modules. These dependencies bound the claim: unknown PA behaviors can be detected from OTA RF windows under controlled calibration regimes while preserving bounded known PA evidence.

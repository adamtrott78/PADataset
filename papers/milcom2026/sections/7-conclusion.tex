This paper introduced DQNGuard, a budgeted open-set decision layer for RF preliminary-action recognition. The method uses predicted-class conditional calibration, variance/energy-style guard evidence, and known-only budgeted thresholding to reject RF windows that do not conform to the learned known-class decision structure. Evaluated on OTA WiFi, Bluetooth, and Zigbee preliminary-action captures, DQNGuard improves unknown-class detection relative to VarMax and a DQN-IDS-style comparison while preserving known-class recognition under a fixed known-rejection budget.

The results also show that surrogate-open calibration is highly target-dependent. The Target--Surrogate Matrix demonstrates that the choice of withheld surrogate class can substantially change unknown F1, indicating that surrogate classes are not interchangeable calibration proxies. This finding motivates future work on principled surrogate selection, pooled surrogate calibration, and larger RF behavior taxonomies that can support stronger leave-multiple-out analyses.

More broadly, the paper positions RF open-set recognition as a sensing layer for QR-CWoS. Known preliminary-action detections can provide precursor evidence for ATT\&CK/EW hypothesis generation, while rejected unknown behaviors can populate a discovery pool for LLM-assisted label-making and continual learning. Future work will connect this OSR layer to larger attack-chain prediction and response-planning systems, evaluate additional RF behaviors and OTA conditions, and develop surrogate-selection mechanisms that remain reliable when the true unknown behavior is unavailable during calibration.

This paper introduced DQNGuard, a budgeted open-set decision layer for RF preliminary-action recognition from OTA captures. DQNGuard operates between a closed-set multi-domain PA backbone and downstream cyber/EMS consumers, using predicted-class calibration, variance/energy-style guard evidence, and known-only budgeted thresholding to decide whether each RF window should be retained as known PA evidence or routed as an unknown behavior.

Across WiFi, Bluetooth, and Zigbee OTA captures, DQNGuard improves the fixed-budget operating point relative to VarMax and a DQN-IDS-style confidence head. The method improves unknown-class detection while constraining known rejection, which is essential when recognized PA evidence must remain useful to downstream analysis. The Target--Surrogate Matrix further shows that surrogate-open calibration is target-dependent: withheld surrogate classes are not interchangeable proxies for future unknown behaviors.

These results support RF open-set recognition as a sensing and triage layer for QR-CWoS rather than as a complete response system. Known PA detections can support precursor-level ATT\&CK/EW hypothesis generation, while rejected unknown behaviors can populate a discovery pool for label-making and continual learning. Future work will focus on principled surrogate selection, pooled calibration, larger RF behavior taxonomies, and tighter integration with downstream attack-chain prediction and response-planning modules.

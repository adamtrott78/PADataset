## Program and application facts

### Application cycle

For the **2027** internship cycle:

- applications open **September 15, 2026**;
- applications close **November 30, 2026**;
- the current FAQ specifies the closing time as **11:59 p.m. Central time**;
- award and non-award notifications are generally sent from January through
  March.

### Program structure

NREIP places college and university students in Department of Navy laboratories
for **10 weeks** of summer research.

The current program page states that an extension of up to four additional weeks
may be possible.

Participants normally work five days per week, 40 hours per week, not to exceed
400 hours over the standard ten-week appointment.

### General eligibility

The current program page states that applicants generally must:

- be enrolled full-time at an accredited four-year college or university,
  subject to stated exceptions;
- meet the applicable citizenship requirements;
- be rising sophomores or equivalent by the internship start;
- graduate no earlier than the spring semester immediately preceding the
  internship unless continuing education satisfies the stated rules; and
- demonstrate interest in subjects relevant to research performed by one or
  more participating laboratories.

Laboratory-specific citizenship and other requirements can be stricter than the
general program rule and must be checked before selecting a laboratory.

### Graduate-student status

The current FAQ defines the graduate NREIP stipend category as requiring the
student, by the start of the internship, to have earned a bachelor's degree and
completed at least one semester of full-time graduate enrollment/coursework
after the bachelor's degree.

This is an application/program fact. Candidate-specific eligibility belongs in
the candidate evidence ledger rather than this file.

### Transcript

A current transcript is required to submit the application. The FAQ states that
either an official or unofficial current transcript may be uploaded.

The transcript is a separate application artifact and is not a substitute for
the resume evidence ledger.

## Selection mechanism

The current NREIP program page identifies these broad selection factors:

- academic achievement;
- personal statements;
- recommendations;
- career interests; and
- research interests.

The current FAQ states that **laboratory research staff** review applications and
make decisions based on:

- academic background;
- research interests;
- personal statement; and
- recommendations.

This means the resume should be designed primarily as evidence for technical and
research reviewers rather than as a federal qualification resume unless the
actual authenticated application imposes a different document contract.

Do not treat this conclusion as permission to omit an application field or
required document. Portal-specific requirements remain controlling when known.

## Laboratory preference and distribution behavior

The current FAQ states that an applicant may select **up to three top laboratory
choices**.

The same FAQ contains two statements whose relationship is not fully explained:

1. after submission, the application is submitted to the Navy laboratories
   selected on the application; and
2. all applications are automatically distributed to all participating labs
   regardless of region, although applicants may identify up to three top lab
   choices.

Preserve this as a public-source ambiguity.

Do not infer that only the three preferred laboratories can review the
application, and do not infer the exact weighting or priority assigned to those
preferences without stronger evidence.

## Initial laboratory screening

The following laboratories emerged from the first current official-source pass
as clearly relevant to computer science, cybersecurity, AI/ML, RF/signal
processing, autonomy, or closely related research.

This is an **unranked screening set**, not the applicant's locked preference
order.

### NUWC Newport — Newport, Rhode Island

Current lab-specific requirements include:

- solely U.S. citizens;
- at least 18 years old before the internship begins; and
- own transportation to the internship site.

Relevant technical areas explicitly described by the lab include:

- Computer Science;
- Cybersecurity;
- Programming;
- communications;
- active and passive signal processing;
- detection, classification, localization, and tracking;
- neural networks;
- time-frequency analysis;
- computer-aided decision making;
- real-time software generation; and
- systems engineering.

NUWC Newport describes itself as a full-spectrum research, development, test and
evaluation, engineering, and fleet-support center for undersea warfare systems.

### NRL Washington — Washington, District of Columbia

The current lab page states that U.S. citizens and permanent residents are
eligible and that dual citizens are eligible.

Relevant divisions described on the NREIP page include:

- **Radar Division (5300)** — research and engineering for RF sensing concepts
  and technologies;
- **Information Technology Division (5500)** — Artificial Intelligence,
  High Assurance Systems, Modeling and Simulation, Virtual Reality,
  Human/Computer Interaction, Computer and Communication Networks,
  Communication Systems, Transmission Technology, and High Performance
  Computing.

NRL describes itself as a broadly multidisciplinary Navy research laboratory
conducting basic research, exploratory development, and advanced technology
development.

### NSWC Dahlgren — Dahlgren, Virginia

Current lab-specific requirements include:

- solely U.S. citizens; and
- at least 18 years old by the internship start.

The current internship description explicitly includes work such as:

- developing software tools for autonomous maritime systems;
- cybersecurity analysis and network monitoring;
- AI/machine-learning applications for naval systems;
- modeling and simulation;
- sensor-related experimentation; and
- prototype development.

The lab also lists Computer Science, Programming, and Software Engineering among
disciplines relevant to interns.

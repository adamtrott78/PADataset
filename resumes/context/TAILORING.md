# Resume tailoring methodology

Status: **EXPERIMENTAL**

This file owns the general method for converting a target opportunity into a
resume content decision.

It does not own candidate facts; see `EVIDENCE.md`.

It does not own the requirements of a particular application; see that
application's `JOB_SPEC.md`.

## Tailoring sequence

For each target opportunity:

1. collect authoritative application/job sources;
2. identify hard eligibility and document constraints;
3. identify who reviews the application and how selection occurs;
4. extract explicit technical, research, communication, and mission signals;
5. distinguish required qualifications from desirable themes;
6. map each meaningful signal to verified candidate evidence;
7. rank evidence by relevance and strength;
8. decide the appropriate resume type and page budget;
9. draft section order and bullet-level content;
10. record intentional omissions;
11. obtain user review;
12. lock the application-specific `RESUME_PLAN.md`;
13. only then begin production/typesetting.

## Selection mechanism matters

Do not treat every resume as the same optimization problem.

Examples:

- a federal qualification resume may need dates, hours/week, eligibility facts,
  and explicit qualification evidence;
- a research-laboratory resume may prioritize publications, research methods,
  technical depth, and project fit;
- a software role may prioritize implementation ownership, systems, languages,
  and delivered artifacts.

The target's actual review mechanism should drive emphasis.

## Evidence mapping

A useful planning table is:

| Target signal | Evidence | Strength | Resume treatment | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|

`Strength` describes evidentiary relevance, not a numerical applicant score.

Never add unsupported keywords simply because they appear in the posting.
Terminology can be aligned when it accurately describes existing evidence.

## Content discipline

Prefer bullets that establish:

- what the candidate did;
- what technical method or system was involved;
- what scale, scope, or constraint mattered;
- what result or artifact was produced;
- why the evidence matters for the target reviewer.

Do not force every bullet to contain all five.

Avoid:

- generic objectives that consume space without adding evidence;
- inflated ownership language;
- unsupported federal/employment relationships;
- stale degree status;
- keyword stuffing;
- unexplained niche jargon when the intended reviewer may not know it.

## Plan-before-layout rule

Do not solve a content problem by shrinking text or silently rewriting during
rendering.

`RESUME_PLAN.md` owns the selected content.

Production should report overflow, density, or hierarchy problems back to the
plan for deliberate resolution.
